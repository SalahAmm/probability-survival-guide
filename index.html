Pattern 01 · CountingVery frequent

How many ways?

In plain English, there are only three counting recipes plus one shortcut. First identify whether order matters and whether repeats are allowed, then pick the matching recipe.

01 Repeats allowed

Slots with repeats

Phone numbers, passwords, PIN codes.

Ask: “How many choices for slot 1? Slot 2?” Then multiply the choices.

Rule Order matters, repeats OK \(n^k\)
02 No repeats

Lineups

Seating people in chairs, arranging distinct objects.

The total number of ways to line up \(n\) people is \(n!\), for example \(4! = 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 24\).

Glued-pairs trick Tape each pair into one block, arrange the blocks, then multiply by the internal swaps: \(k!(2!)^k\)
03 Simultaneous

Grab a handful

“Draw 5 at once” or any selection where order does not matter.

Use combinations. The nCr button on your calculator does the arithmetic.

Combinations \[ \binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!} \]
\[ P = \frac{\text{winning combinations}}{\text{total combinations}} \]
04 Shortcut

The “opposite” move

“At least one,” “not all the same,” or “different colors.”

Instead of counting ten messy cases, count only the exact opposite once.

Complement \[ P(\text{what you want}) = 1 - P(\text{exact opposite}) \]
Fast diagnosis

Repeats allowed? Use \(n^k\). No repeats and order matters? Use \(n!\). No repeats and order does not matter? Use \(\binom{n}{k}\).

Pattern 02 · Probability treesVery frequent

Trees, total probability & Bayes

Whenever a story gives you groups — Factory A/B/C, spam/not-spam, routed through Medea/not — and then a test result inside each group, immediately draw a two-step tree.

01

Walk along a branch

Multiply the probabilities along the path to get the probability of the whole branch.

\[ P(A \cap D) = P(A)\,P(D\mid A) \]
02

Add the endings

Add every branch ending at the same result. This is the Law of Total Probability.

\[ P(D) = \sum_i P(A_i)P(D\mid A_i) \]
03

Work backwards

Given that \(D\) happened, put the branch through \(A_i\) over the total probability of \(D\).

\[ P(A_i\mid D) = \frac{P(A_i\cap D)}{P(D)} \]
Presentation tip

Always draw the tree before touching the calculator. Label every branch with its probability, then write the total \(P(D)\) in the middle. Bayes problems become a simple “part over whole” calculation.

2A

The spam filter

Assessment 4 · Exercise 1

  • 50% of emails are spam\(P(S)=0.50,\; P(\bar S)=0.50\)
  • The filter catches 99% of spam\(P(D\mid S)=0.99\)
  • False positive rate\(P(D\mid \bar S)=0.05\)

An email is detected as spam. What is the probability that it is actually non-spam?

  1. 1

    Total probability of detection \(D\).

    \[ P(D) = (0.50 \times 0.99) + (0.50 \times 0.05) = 0.495 + 0.025 = \mathbf{0.52} \]
  2. 2

    Bayes: use the non-spam branch over the total.

    \[ P(\bar S \mid D) = \frac{0.025}{0.52} = \mathbf{\frac{5}{104}} \approx 0.0481 \;\;(\mathbf{4.81\%}) \]

Even with a 99% accurate spam detector, about 1 in 20 flagged emails can still be legitimate when the spam base rate is only 50%.

Internet packets routed through Medea

Assessment 3 · Exercise 2

    Probability that a packet is dropped.

Pattern 03 · Discrete random variablesBiggest point-maker

The random variable scoreboard

A random variable \(X\) is simply a number attached to a random experiment — like “sum of two balls” or “money won.” Every exam question about \(X\) comes back to four ideas.

01

The PMF

A table of every value and its probability.

The probabilities must add to exactly 1. If an unknown \(\alpha\) appears, set the total equal to 1 and solve.

\[ \sum_i p_i = 1 \]
\(x_i\)\(x_1\)\(x_2\)\(x_3\) \(p_i\)\(p_1\)\(p_2\)\(p_3\)
02

The CDF

The running-total staircase.

\(F(x)=P(X\le x)\) starts at 0 before the smallest value and ends at 1 at the largest. Write every interval as \(a \le x < b\).

\[ P(X=x_i) = F(x_i)-F(x_{i-1}) \]

Going backwards? The jumps are the possible values; each probability is the jump size.

03

Average & spread

Expectation and variance.

\[ E(X)=\sum_i x_i p_i \qquad E(X^2)=\sum_i x_i^2 p_i \]
\[ \operatorname{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2 \qquad \sigma(X)=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)} \]

Square only \(x_i\), never \(p_i\). For \(Y=aX+b\): \(E(Y)=aE(X)+b\) and \(\operatorname{Var}(Y)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)\).

04

Tricky intervals

Where most clean solutions lose a mark.

  • Strictly less\(P(X<c)\)Add probabilities below \(c\) only.
  • Strictly greater\(P(X>c)=1-F(c)\)The opposite of \(X\le c\).
  • Between\(P(a<X\le b)=F(b)-F(a)\)Exclude \(a\), include \(b\).

Pattern 04 · Named distributions5 of 6 assessments

Binomial vs. Poisson in five seconds

Both are “count how many events” distributions. The difference is what the story gives you: a fixed number of Yes/No trials, or an average rate over a continuous interval.

Fixed tries B

Binomial \(X\sim\mathcal{B}(n,p)\)

“We pick \(n\) people or items, and each one independently has probability \(p\) of Yes.”

\[ P(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} \]
Mean\(E(X)=np\) Variance\(\operatorname{Var}(X)=np(1-p)\)
Average rate P

Poisson \(X\sim\mathcal{P}(\lambda)\)

“Events happen randomly at an average rate \(\lambda\) per minute, page, hour, or other interval.”

\[ P(X=k)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!} \]
Mean\(E(X)=\lambda\) Variance\(\operatorname{Var}(X)=\lambda\)
Time / space scaling

If the rate is 2 calls per minute and the question asks about 3 minutes, multiply: \(\lambda=2\times 3=6\). Never use the per-minute rate for a longer interval.

The secret bridge

When \(n\ge 30\), \(p\le0.1\), and \(np<5\), approximate a Binomial with a Poisson: \(\mathcal{B}(n,p)\approx\mathcal{P}(\lambda=np)\). Exact Binomial still works on a calculator.

Eight mistakes that cost people 18/20

These are not deep conceptual gaps. They are small slips that turn a correct method into a wrong final answer. Read them again on the way into the exam.

01

“At least 2” is not \(1-P(X\le2)\)

Wrong\(P(X\ge2)=1-P(X\le2)\)

Right\(P(X\ge2)=1-P(X\le1)\)

“At least 2” includes 2, so its opposite is “0 or 1.”

02

Squaring probabilities in \(E(X^2)\)

Wrong\(\sum x_i^2p_i^2\)

Right\(\sum x_i^2p_i\)

Square only \(x_i\), never \(p_i\). Then subtract \([E(X)]^2\) at the end.

03

Confusing \(P(X<2)\) with \(P(X\le2)\)

Wrong\(P(X<2)=F(2)\)

Right\(P(X<2)=F(2)-P(X=2)\)

\(F(2)\) includes \(X=2\). For \(P(X<2)\), stop right before 2.

04

Mixing up \(P(A\mid B)\) and \(P(B\mid A)\)

WrongPutting the known condition on top

Right“Given…” always goes after the bar

The condition becomes the denominator in Bayes. Read “if,” “given that,” and “knowing that” slowly.

05

Forgetting to scale \(\lambda\) in Poisson

WrongUsing “2 per minute” for a 3-minute question

Right\(\lambda=2\times3=6\)

The rate must match the interval in the question. Minutes, pages, hours — scale every time.

06

Forgetting \(0!=1\) and \(a^0=1\)

WrongWriting \(P(X=0)=0\)

Right\(P(X=0)=e^{-\lambda}\)

In \(P(X=0)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^0}{0!}\), the power and factorial are both 1.

07

Forgetting the denominator drops

WrongSecond draw still out of 7

RightWithout replacement: 6 balls left

If you start with 7 balls and draw without replacement, the second branch is out of 6.

08

Not flipping the inequality sign

Wrong\(-0.7n<-9.21\Rightarrow n<13.16\)

Right\(n>13.16\Rightarrow n=14\)

Dividing by a negative number flips \(<\) to \(>\). Then round up for a minimum count.

The cheat sheet

Everything above, compressed into four blocks. Read it last before you walk in.

01

Counting

  • Codes — repeats OK, order matters\(n^k\)
  • Lineup — no repeats, order matters\(n!\)
  • Glued pairs — \(k\) pairs\(k!(2!)^k\)
  • Grab a handful — order does not matter\(\binom{n}{k}=\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\)
02

Probability & Bayes

  • Complement\(P(\bar A)=1-P(A)\)
  • Union\(P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)\)
  • Independent?\(P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)\)
  • Total probability\(P(D)=\sum P(A_i)P(D\mid A_i)\)
  • Bayes\(P(A_i\mid D)=\frac{P(A_i)P(D\mid A_i)}{P(D)}\)
03

Discrete random variable

  • Total probability\(\sum p_i=1\)
  • CDF\(F(x)=P(X\le x)\)
  • Interval\(P(a<X\le b)=F(b)-F(a)\)
  • Upper tail\(P(X>a)=1-F(a)\)
  • Mean\(E(X)=\sum x_ip_i\)
  • Second moment\(E(X^2)=\sum x_i^2p_i\)
  • Variance\(\operatorname{Var}(X)=E(X^2)-[E(X)]^2\)
  • Standard deviation\(\sigma(X)=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}\)
  • Linear transform\(E(aX+b)=aE(X)+b\)
  • Variance transform\(\operatorname{Var}(aX+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(X)\)
04

Named distributions

Binomial \(\mathcal{B}(n,p)\)fixed \(n\) tries, Yes/No with probability \(p\)
\[ P(X=k)=\binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k} \]

\(E(X)=np\)  ·  \(\operatorname{Var}(X)=np(1-p)\)

Poisson \(\mathcal{P}(\lambda)\)average rate \(\lambda\) per interval — scale if time changes
\[ P(X=k)=e^{-\lambda}\frac{\lambda^k}{k!} \]

\(E(X)=\lambda\)  ·  \(\operatorname{Var}(X)=\lambda\)

Approximation bridge If \(n\ge30\), \(p\le0.1\), and \(np<5\), then \(\mathcal{B}(n,p)\approx\mathcal{P}(\lambda=np)\).
